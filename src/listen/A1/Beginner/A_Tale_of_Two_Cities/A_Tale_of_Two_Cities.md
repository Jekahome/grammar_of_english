<p class="first-para">It was the year 1775. A coach was going from London to Dover. The road was wet and muddy. The horses pulled the heavy coach slowly. A man on a horse came along the road behind the coach. He was riding quickly. Stop! shouted the rider. What do you want? asked the coach driver. I have a message. shouted the rider. He stopped his horse in front of the coach. The coach also stopped. The message is for Mr. Jarvis Lorry, said the rider. A man looked out of the window of the coach. He was about 60 years old, and he wore old-fashioned clothes.</p>
<p>He saw the rider and asked, what news do you bring, Jerry? Do you know this man, sir?" asked the coach-driver. There are robbers on this road. I know him. Replied the old man. His name is Jerry Cruncher. He has come from my bank. Jerry Cruncher is a messenger, not a robber. Here is a letter for you, Mr. Lorry, the messenger said. Mr. Tellson's wants you to wait at the Royal George Inn at Dover. A young lady will travel to Paris with you." Thank you, Jerry. Mr. Lorry said. He took the letter.</p>
<p>I will wait in Dover. Good night. At the top of the letter were the words, Tellson's Bank, Temple Bar, London. The date on the letter was the 18th of November, 1775. To Mr. Jarvis Lorry, the letter said, We have news from Tellson's Bank in Paris. Dr. Manette is alive. He is living at the wine shop of Monsieur and Madame Defarge in Saint Antoine, Paris. Dr. Manette is ill. He was in prison for 18 years. Lucy Manette, Dr. Manette's daughter, will meet you in Dover. Go to the Royal George Inn in Dover.</p>
<p>Wait for Lucy Manette there. Lucy has never met her father. Take Lucy to Paris with you. Then bring Dr. Manette and Lucy back to London. The name at the bottom of the letter was Tellson's. Mr. Lorry waited at the Royal George Inn at Dover. Miss Lucy Manette arrived the next day. She was about 18 years old and she had long golden hair. Miss Manette, said Mr. Lorry. I work for Tellson's Bank. There is a Tellson's Bank in London and a Tellson's Bank in Paris. I often travel between the two cities.</p>
<p>Twenty years ago, your father came to Tellson's Bank in Paris, said Mr. Lorry. He left some money in the bank. Yes. said Lucy Manette. My father went to prison, and he died. My mother told me about Tellson's bank. My mother brought me to England. I was very young. I have lived in England all my life. But my mother taught me French. My mother died a few years ago, Lucy said. And Tellson's bank took care of me. Your father went to prison, Mr. Lorry said. But he did not go to court.</p>
<p>There was no trial. That is right, said Lucy. My father died in prison. No, my dear, said Mr. Lorry. I have news of your father. He did not die in prison. He is alive, and he is living in Paris. I will take you to him." Lucy Manette put her hand to her face. Miss Pross! She said loudly. Then she fainted. She fell into Mr. Lorry's arms. A large woman with red hair ran into the room. I will take care of her! She shouted. I am Miss Pross. Miss Manette's companion.</p>

This is Miss Manette.</p>
<p>Where is Doctor Manette? We're going to take him to England. He is ill, said Monsieur Defarge. My wife is taking care of him. Many years ago I was Doctor Manette's servant. He helped us then. She was knitting with wool. Her face was hard and unkind. This is Mr. Lorry, said Monsieur Defarge. He wants to see Dr. Manette.</p>
<p>Dr. Manette will not know you, said Madame Defarge. He was in prison for eighteen years. He remembers nothing. Madam Defarge looked at Lucy. "'Are you his daughter?" she asked. "'Yes,' said Lucy. <p>One hundred and five, North Tower. He replied. He knows his number in the Bastille prison. Said Madame Defarge. But he does not know his name. Mr. Lorry was a kind man. Come with us, Dr. Manette. He said quietly. Your daughter is here. We will take you to England. Dr. Manette did not understand. Mr. Lorry held the old man's arm. He led him out of the room. Dr. Manette walked slowly. He held a pair of shoes in his hand. Mr. Lorry took Dr. Manette, Miss Pross, and Lucy back to England. They went by ship from Calais to Dover.</p>
<p>On the ship, Dr. Manette was weak and ill. A young man helped Lucy and Mr. Lorry. They took care of Dr. Manette. You are kind, sir. Lucy said to the young man. What is your name? My father has been ill for a long time, said Lucy, but he will get well in England. We will meet again, said Darnay. I will visit you in England."</p>
<p>In England, Dr. Manette slowly got well. After many months, he started to remember things. He remembered his wife, he remembered his daughter. But he did not remember the Bastille prison, and he did not remember his journey to England. Lucy and her father lived in a small house in London. Miss Pross lived with them. She took care of the house. Mr. Lorry became a friend of the Manettes and Miss Pross. He often visited them.</p>
<p>One day, Dr. Manette and Lucy received a letter. It was a letter from a lawyer. You must come to the Old Bailey law court on Monday, the letter said. You must speak at the trial of Charles Darnay.</p>

Mr. Stryver was right.</p>
<p>The twelve men of the jury left the courtroom. They had to make a decision. Was Darnay guilty? Or was he not guilty? A few hours later, the jury came back into the courtroom. The judge spoke to one of the jurymen. What is your decision? He asked. Not guilty, said the juryman. Darnay is innocent. He is not a spy.</p>
